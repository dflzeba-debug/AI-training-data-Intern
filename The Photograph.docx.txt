    {\Huge\fontfamily{qzc}\selectfont\color{magenta-text}\textbf{A Photograph}}
    
    \vspace{0.5em}
    \hfill {\Large\fontfamily{pzc}\selectfont Shirley Toulson}
    
    \vspace{1.5em}
    \rule{\linewidth}{0.5pt}
    \vspace{2em}

    % Poem Stanzas
    \noindent The cardboard shows me how it was\\
    When the two girl cousins went paddling,\\
    Each one holding one of my mother’s hands,\\
    And she the big girl --- some twelve years or so.\\
    All three stood still to smile through their hair\\
    At the uncle with the camera. A sweet face,\\
    My mother’s, that was before I was born.\\
    And the sea, which appears to have changed less,\\
    Washed their terribly transient feet.
    
    \vspace{1.5em}
    
    \noindent Some twenty --- thirty --- years later\\
    She’d laugh at the snapshot. ``See Betty\\
    And Dolly,'' she’d say, ``and look how they\\
    Dressed us for the beach.'' The sea holiday\\
    Was her past, mine is her laughter. Both wry\\
    With the laboured ease of loss.
    
    \vspace{1.5em}
    
    \noindent Now she’s been dead nearly as many years\\
    As that girl lived. And of this circumstance\\
    There is nothing to say at all.\\
    Its silence silences.

    \vspace{3em}

    {\Large\color{black!80} Think it out \hspace{0.2em}\color{magenta-accent!60}$\blacksquare$}
    
    \vspace{1.5em}
    
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item What does the word `cardboard' denote in the poem? Why has this word been used?
        \item What has the camera captured?
        \item What has not changed over the years? Does this suggest something to you?
        \item The poet's mother laughed at the snapshot. What did this laugh indicate?
        \item What is the meaning of the line ``Both wry with the laboured ease of loss.''
        \item What does ``this circumstance'' refer to?
        \item The three stanzas depict three different phases. What are they?
    \end{enumerate}

            \noindent Poems are included to heighten students' sensitivity to literary writing and to appreciate rhythm and sound patterns in language. Follow these steps:
            \begin{itemize}
                \item Read the poem aloud once without the students looking at the poem. Ask them a few general questions.
                \item Re-read the poem with the students looking at the poem. Ask a few more questions to check comprehension.
